Purity & ICAR-NISA

People compromise on quality without knowing it, because they have no way to judge it. Shubharambh asked government scientists to define how an original product is identified, and to test ours. This page explains who does what, how each product is tested, and how you can check for yourself.

Who is ICAR-NISA

A century of work on lac, gums and resins

The ICAR–National Institute of Secondary Agriculture (NISA) is part of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research, Government of India, at Namkum, Ranchi (Jharkhand).

  • 1924: founded as the Indian Lac Research Institute (the lah sansthan)
  • 2007: renamed the Indian Institute of Natural Resins and Gums (IINRG), covering lac, gum karaya, guar, guggul, resins and more
  • 2022: renamed NISA. Rather than create a new institute, ICAR merged the mandate into IINRG, which already had land, buildings and laboratories, so that work could start quickly
  • A mandate to raise farmers’ income from secondary agriculture: by-products of rice, wheat, honey, forests and livestock
  • Home to eastern India’s first regional honey-testing laboratory, set up under the National Beekeeping & Honey Mission

Paperless proof

From batch to QR code

Printing every batch report would be bulky and waste paper. Each report goes on ICAR-NISA’s official website instead, and a report on a government site is more credible than one we print ourselves.

Honey: certification live

How honey purity is measured

Honey purity is measured on about 17 to 18 parameters. One of them is pollen, which shows which flower the honey came from. If the dominant pollen belongs to one flower, the honey can be declared moringa honey, tulsi honey or karanj honey. Indian honey had rarely been certified this way before. FSSAI’s 2020 honey standard requires at least 45% pollen from the declared plant for a monofloral label, and Shubharambh aims for 60% or more.

Why routine tests weren’t enough

In 2020 a Centre for Science and Environment investigation found that 17 of 22 honey samples, including major brands, passed India’s routine tests but failed NMR. They contained sugar syrups designed to evade detection. FSSAI then added specific markers for rice syrup (SMR, TMR) and foreign oligosaccharides, and advanced labs use NMR profiling as an internationally accepted fingerprint.

Raw, ripe honey keeps for years

In raw honey, the bees have already dried out the moisture in the comb. Such ripe honey has a very long shelf life: it may darken with time, but it does not spoil. Crystallisation is also natural, especially in mustard and sunflower honey, and is not a sign of sugar.

Product by product

What ICAR-NISA checks

Each family of products has its own identity markers and adulterants. These are the methods used. Every product page lists its specific tests.

Bhimseni kapoor & fresheners

In progress
  • Optical rotation: natural camphor is dextrorotatory (about +41° to +43°), while synthetic camphor from turpentine is racemic (≈0°)
  • Melting point (~175–179 °C) and non-volatile residue (should be nil)
  • GC-MS to detect naphthalene, paradichlorobenzene, paraffin and solvents
  • Specific gravity: camphor ≈ 0.99 floats, naphthalene ≈ 1.15 sinks

Bhimseni Kapoor · Tikia Kapoor · Fresheners

Guggul, dhuna, loban, sambrani

In progress
  • HPLC fingerprint: E- & Z-guggulsterone confirm true guggul and expose look-alike gums
  • Boswellic acids for loban, and benzoic/cinnamic acids for sambrani (benzoin)
  • Liebermann–Storch test for rosin (colophony), the commonest adulterant
  • Acid value, softening point, ash and foreign matter (plastic, sugar, bark)

Gugol · Dhuwan · Loban · Sambrani

Dhoop cups, cones & sticks

In progress
  • Fixed-carbon test to detect charcoal powder
  • GC-MS screen for diethyl phthalate and synthetic aroma chemicals
  • Resin marker tests (guggul, loban) and ash profile of the cow-dung base
  • Burn chamber: burn time, soot and smoke profile

Gugul Cup · Dhup Cone · Dhup Sticks

Haldi-ashtagandh & roli

In progress
  • Curcumin content (HPLC/UV) confirms real turmeric
  • Dye screen for metanil yellow, Sudan dyes and Rhodamine B
  • Lead, chromium & mercury by ICP-MS (lead chromate, red lead)
  • Starch (iodine) and acid-insoluble ash to detect arrowroot, dolomite and talc

Haldi Ashtagandh · Roli

Gond katira, dhawda gond, hing

In progress
  • Swelling index & viscosity (gum grade), and volatile acidity to identify gum karaya
  • Identity (karaya, tragacanth, ghatti or acacia) by physico-chemical profile
  • Hing: starch, gum arabic, rosin and volatile oil to separate pure resin from compounded hing
  • Microbial load, ash, heavy metals

Gond Katira · Dhawda Gond · Hing

A2 ghee, sendha & kala namak

In progress
  • Ghee: BR reading (~40–43 at 40 °C), RM & Polenske values, Baudouin test (vanaspati), β-sitosterol, GC fatty-acid profile, and herd/breed verification for the A2 claim
  • Rock salt: NaCl, insoluble matter, mineral profile and added-colour check
  • Kala namak: sulphide/sulphate markers of the kiln process and iron
  • Heavy metals for all

An honest note on honey

The thumb test, water-glass test, flame test and ant test cannot detect modern syrup adulteration. For honey, the only reliable check is a lab report, which is why every Shubharambh honey links to its ICAR-NISA batch report.
