Shubharambh means an auspicious beginning. We chose the name because we wanted to start again with things India once knew well.

How it began

From herbs and smoke to a question about purity

Shubharambh started with a simple idea: why not work with the herbs and natural materials that nature gave us, and with the environment? In earlier times, homes were kept free of germs with jadi-buti and smoke: gums like guggul, dhuna, and dhuwan, the resin of the Sal tree. These are natural practices, but the Government of India and researchers worldwide have hardly studied them.

That raised a harder question. Take Bhimseni camphor, which has many properties. How do we explore them? Where do we get certification, when synthetic material comes in from abroad and nobody can tell which is original? People compromise on quality without knowing it, because they have no way to judge it.

So we knocked on the government’s door and asked its scientists to tell us how the original can be identified, and which of its features actually work.

The journey to ICAR-NISA

  • Gums and resins are agricultural and forest produce, so the natural partner was ICAR.
  • ICAR’s institute for gums and resins was the IINRG at Namkum, Ranchi, which began life as the Indian Lac Research Institute (the lah sansthan).
  • In 2022, ICAR created the National Institute of Secondary Agriculture (NISA). Since IINRG already had land, buildings and labs, it was renamed NISA so that work could start quickly.
  • NISA opened a much wider field: not just gums but rice, wheat and other produce. The question became how farmers can earn more from by-products.
  • Shubharambh and ICAR-NISA signed an MOU. ICAR-NISA handles quality and testing, and Shubharambh handles production, marketing and sales.
The honey example

Honey is the cheapest thing a hive produces

A farmer who keeps bees usually sells only honey and doesn’t know what else the hive can give. Secondary agriculture changes that.

Wax

From the comb comes wax for foundation sheets (so bees fill honey faster), natural candles, balms and lip care, with no petroleum and scented with essential oils.

Pure beeswax →

Royal jelly

It sells for more than honey and wax and is used in making medicines.

Pollen

A trap at the hive entrance collects the pollen packed on bees’ legs. It is extremely protein-rich and sells for thousands of rupees a kilo.

Beepolyn →

Venom

A very mild electric pulse makes bees sting a glass plate, and the collected venom is used in arthritis research and medicine. It is among the most valuable hive products.

Trained farmers can earn from all of these. ICAR-NISA is also working on honey authenticity, measured on about 17–18 parameters including pollen, so that a jar can honestly be called moringa, tulsi or karanj honey. Indian honey had rarely been certified this way before.

Mission · Vision

What we are building

Mission

Bring back the natural, original products of the Indian household with proof of purity from ICAR-NISA. Raise production with farmers, forest collectors, beekeepers and gaushalas so they earn more. Replace charcoal, naphthalene and synthetic dyes with cow dung, gums, herbs and resins, so the home environment is genuinely cleaner.

Vision

Old people who can tell the original at a glance are still with us. In ten years they may not be, and a generation could forget how to recognise anything original. Our vision is an India where that knowledge lives on in daily use, and where every gift given is handmade in India.
