What is Shubharambh

The name means an auspicious beginning. For us it means starting again.

Our grandparents could tell real camphor from fake by its smell, knew that haldi and chuna make roli, and kept gond katira for the summer. They burned guggul and dhuna in the evening to clean the air of the house. Much of that knowledge has slipped out of the Indian household. In another ten years, the founder warns, people may not be able to recognise an original item at all.

Shubharambh brings those things back slowly and honestly. We find genuine sources, raise production with farmers and forest collectors, and put the products in front of the public with a clear account of what they are and how their purity was checked.

The second half of Shubharambh is Indian handicrafts: affordable, handmade gifts from different states (minakari, marble, glass, wood, terracotta, iron, ceramic and bamboo). Buying them supports Indian MSMEs instead of imports, and it moves one state’s culture into another state’s home.

Environment clarity · Germ protection

Purifying the air the way Indian homes once did, and checking it honestly

In earlier times, homes were cleaned of germs with herbs and smoke rather than chemicals. Gums and resins such as guggul, dhuna and dhuwan (the resin of the Sal tree) were burned on cow-dung fire, and Bhimseni camphor was diffused through the rooms. The founder points out that this natural practice has hardly been researched by the Government of India or anyone else. That is why Shubharambh took it to the scientists of ICAR-NISA.

What has been studied

A 2007 peer-reviewed study (Nautiyal, Chauhan & Nene, Journal of Ethnopharmacology 114:446) found that one hour of medicinal smoke from havan samagri reduced airborne bacteria in a closed room by over 94%. The effect lasted up to 24 hours, and some pathogenic bacteria were still absent after 30 days.

What is tradition

Ayurveda and folk practice describe camphor, guggul, loban, neem and tulsi as air-purifiers and insect-repellents, and the purpose of a havan as cleaning the environment. Some texts say camphor increases oxygen. We share these as traditional beliefs and do not claim them as proven facts.

What is being researched

Under our MOU, ICAR-NISA scientists are studying the features of Bhimseni camphor, natural resins and our dhoop formulations. Research takes time. When results are published, we will link them here and on every product page.

What has crept into Indian homes

  • Naphthalene balls in wardrobes: toxic, and linked to haemolytic anaemia in children
  • Charcoal-and-bamboo agarbatti with synthetic perfume, filling small puja rooms with fine particulates
  • Havan samagri padded with sawdust and waste, so it no longer cleans anything
  • Aerosol sprays and chemical vaporizers running all night in bedrooms

What Shubharambh puts back

  • Bhimseni kapoor and flavoured fresheners in place of naphthalene
  • Guggul cups, cones and bambooless sticks on a cow-dung base with no charcoal
  • Pure sal-resin dhuwan, loban and guggul for genuine havan
  • A gaumutra vaporizer with no synthetic insecticide

Any smoke, natural or not, should be used in ventilated rooms and kept away from infants and people with asthma. We say this on every product.

What the Indian household is missing

Knowledge that is disappearing from our homes

Ask a child today whether they know sendha namak. Most won’t, though it is full of minerals. We buy haldi without checking whether it contains any curcumin, and the hing on our shelf can be mostly wheat flour. Here is what has quietly changed.

Our quality partner

ICAR-NISA tests the products, and Shubharambh brings them to you

Shubharambh approached the Government of India’s agricultural research system for proof that would let people trust an original product. The ICAR–National Institute of Secondary Agriculture (NISA) at Namkum, Ranchi, was the right home. It began in 1924 as the Indian Lac Research Institute, later became the Indian Institute of Natural Resins and Gums (IINRG), and in 2022 was renamed NISA with a wider mandate: helping farmers earn more from secondary agricultural produce and by-products.

Try it at home

The haldi + chuna test

Rub a little haldi with chuna (edible lime) and a few drops of lemon juice, then keep it in the sun. Within half an hour, real turmeric turns from yellow to red, because curcumin changes colour in an alkaline medium. No chemicals are involved. This is exactly how traditional roli is made.

Market roli is often made from arrowroot or dolomite powder with synthetic dye. It doesn’t need turmeric at all, because it is cheaper that way. Our purity page lists more checks like this for camphor, ghee, hing, rock salt and gond katira.

One nation, many crafts

Use a Gujarat craft in Jharkhand, and a Jharkhand craft in Gujarat

Each state, district and village in India has its own craft: Jharkhand’s Dokra, Bihar’s GI-tagged Madhubani, Gujarat’s torans and meenakari, Rajasthan’s marble, the glass of Firozabad and the metalwork of Moradabad. A small artisan cannot market across the country, so Shubharambh brings these crafts onto one platform and distributes them from state to state.

The economy stays in India, MSMEs grow stronger, and we come to understand each other’s cultures.
